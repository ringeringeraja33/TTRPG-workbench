# Dream Archaeology 0.2 — minimum playable rules

An original cooperative prototype for one GM and two players. You reconstruct four damaged dream archives while deciding which memories to preserve. Use six memory cards, two character sheets, one d6 and a five-box scene action track. Discuss acceptable memory-loss themes before play. Players choose actions and feelings; the GM portrays the archives and states opportunities and consequences. Out-of-character pauses have no game cost.

## Creation — R-create revision 2

Each player writes a name, motivation, link to the other archaeologist and three personal memories. Give each memory a unique ID and a sentence. All start fresh; every memory card remains in play when faded. Assign 2, 1 and 0 once each to Trace (interpret evidence), Weave (repair material dream structures), and Listen (negotiate with echoes). These numbers are not spendable points. Strain starts at 0 with a maximum of 6. Take a notebook, chalk and thread. Equipment is owned, has no numeric bonus and is neither consumed nor sold in this prototype. There is no money or encumbrance subsystem. Memory cards are the only supernatural boosts.

## Risky action — R-delve revision 2

Trigger: the player pursues a scene's archive through an uncertain, consequential action. The player states intent and method; the GM identifies a supported approach and confirms access. All three approaches have opportunities in each sample scene. For a safe feasible act, simply describe its outcome; it adds no archive progress and spends no mechanical turn. Impossible methods require a changed situation; do not roll for them.

Before rolling, name zero, one or two fresh memory IDs you own. Fade them immediately; each grants +1 to this action. Add any one pending help bonus, consuming it. Roll 1d6, add the approach rating, spent memories and help. A total of 5 or more gives 2 archive progress and no strain; 3–4 gives 1 progress and 1 strain; 1–2 gives no progress and 2 strain. Progress caps at 5; strain caps at 6. All arithmetic is integer; no rounding and no natural die exceptions. Resolve both progress and strain before the scene deadline. A failed action advances the action track and the GM reveals a damaged fragment with one specified uncertainty; it never erases an already known fact.

There are no opposed rolls, attacks of opportunity, damage dice or critical hits. Opposition is the announced obstacle and deadline. A retry is a new turn with new costs; it does not refund memories. Cancellation before confirming legal inputs spends nothing; after a legal roll all costs stand. A character at strain 6 is fractured and cannot delve or help; they can recover, trade or wait. Player-versus-player coercion is outside this cooperative prototype.

Ordinary example: Trace 2, die 2, one memory and help 1 totals 6: +2 progress, no strain, memory faded, help consumed. Boundary: Listen 1 plus die 1 with strain 5 yields 2, no progress, strain becomes 6; the player chooses how the fracture appears. If progress was already 5, further legal investigation still consumes time and may cause strain; no extra archive reward is created.

## Memory exchange — R-memory revision 2

On your turn, exchange exactly one owned memory card for one owned by the other player, with both players' consent. This consumes one turn total. Card ID, text and fresh/faded status move together. A card can only be spent if fresh and owned; it cannot be duplicated, split, or refreshed by trade. Naming a faded card or spending the same ID twice is illegal. There is no memory regeneration during this expedition. A character with no fresh cards can still delve with zero spend. The new custodian decides what the borrowed story means to them; no automatic attribute changes occur.

Example: A trades faded A-1 for fresh B-2; A can spend B-2 later, B cannot spend A-1. Trading them back restores ownership only, not freshness. This allows sharing finite reserves without multiplying them. Any promise to return a card is fictional, never an automatic second transfer.

## Help and recovery — R-support / R-strain revision 2

Help uses your turn, fades one fresh card you own, and gives the other nonfractured character +1 on their next delve this scene. State how your memory supports their method. Pending help does not stack: giving help to a character already helped is illegal with no payment. It can combine with up to two self-spent memories. Help expires at the end of the scene with no refund. A helper cannot help themselves.

Recover uses your turn to reduce your own strain by 2, minimum 0. Each character may do this once per scene, even if already at 0; note its use. Narrate a grounding detail. This works at 6 strain and removes fractured status by reducing strain to 4. Recovery does not refresh memories or help. Wait consumes a turn and changes no resource. Thus even a fractured party with spent recovery can finish the scene without a rules deadlock. Waiting sacrifices its remaining opportunity.

Boundary: at strain 1, recover results in 0; a second recover that scene is illegal. A help payment never earns progress by itself. These choices compete for the same five scene turns.

## Scenes, failure and ending — R-scene revision 2

An expedition has four scenes, each with exactly five total player turns. Players agree who acts; the GM invites a waiting player to act but cannot assign a character's decisions. Each accepted delve/help/recover/trade/wait consumes one turn. There are no enemy turns. Invalid inputs are corrected before consuming a turn. At the fifth turn, resolve all consequences first, then compare progress with 5. At 5, recover a complete archive; below 5, recover a damaged fragment and the GM gives the scenario's onward route with its explicit missing context. A failed scene never traps the party. Progress overflow does not carry into the next scene.

Start the next scene at progress 0 with help cleared and both recovery allowances restored. Strain and all memory ownership/faded states persist. After four scenes, the expedition ends: three or four complete archives yield a coherent reconstruction; two yield a contested reconstruction; zero or one yields only fragments. Players describe what they preserve or relinquish. A party may end early by mutual agreement, applying the ending to archives already complete; no more action rolls then occur.

## Growth — R-grow revision 2

At expedition end with at least two complete archives, each player may increase exactly one approach below 2 by 1, once. This grants neither memories nor strain recovery. Record the expedition's growth as claimed even if declining it. At fewer than two archives, no numerical growth occurs; still describe a learned connection. A later campaign needs an explicit new version for preparation and memory replacement; this rulebook ends after the current expedition and its growth step.

## GM and content reference

Prepare four named obstacles with sensory descriptions, one route for each approach, a recoverable truth, and a damaged-fragment alternative. Keep hidden truths in a GM file. Reveal action costs and progress before commitment; disclose relevant consequences without solving the mystery for players. Avoid requiring a specific memory's text as the only route. Violence against a dream construct can be a Weave method if it achieves the stated goal; it gains no unlisted damage or extra action. For material ambiguity, record a provisional ruling before spending, or choose an established legal action and return to design after the scene.
