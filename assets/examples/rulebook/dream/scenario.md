# GM only — The Harbor That Forgot Its Name

Original synthetic scenario. Do not give this file to players. Four scenes each last five shared actions under R-scene. No mandatory skill or die result is required to receive an onward route. The core player brief: an erased harbor record has left families uncertain which shore they came from.

| Scene | Sensory opening and obstacle | Trace / Weave / Listen opportunities | Complete truth | Damaged fragment and onward route |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Salt index | Names dissolve on damp shelving | Compare tide marks / bind wet pages / question a librarian echo | A ferry carried the records inland | A ferry seal survives; follow its corridor without the passenger list |
| 2. Glass ferry | The cabin is split across reflections | Compare tickets / bridge the floor / bargain with a conductor | The evacuation was voluntary | A dock sign points to the bell room; the evacuation motive remains uncertain |
| 3. Bell room | Each note overwrites the previous one | Transcribe intervals / repair a clapper / hear the keeper | A keeper hid names to protect refugees | A broken nameplate points to the final cabinet; authorship remains unknown |
| 4. Cabinet of shores | Drawers open onto incompatible coastlines | Cross-reference dates / rebind compartments / negotiate with witnesses | Two histories can be preserved together | Keep one contradictory testimony; end with explicitly incomplete knowledge |

Failed delves expose one incomplete sensory clue from the relevant row. State its uncertainty. Do not silently reveal a complete truth from a failed scene. The scene-three failed sample establishes the need to recover at the next scene's start. No combat stats are needed; threatening echoes offer obstacles resolved with the same procedure.

The sample chooses a contested ending with two complete archives. Ask each player what changed in their relationship to a faded or borrowed memory. Never narrate an unwanted belief for them. Tool traces are GM records and are not player exports.
